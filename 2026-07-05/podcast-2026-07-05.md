Alex: Next up, a paper that fits right into this world: Abhiram Ravikumar shared an arXiv paper called Ask, Don't Judge: Binary Questions for Interpretable LLM Evaluation and Self-Improvement.
Maya: I like the title already. The idea is simple: instead of one fuzzy judge score, break evaluation into lots of yes-or-no questions.

Alex: That’s the framework called BINEVAL. It asks atomic binary questions, then combines the answers into a score.
Maya: In plain English, that means the evaluator becomes easier to inspect. You can see exactly why an answer was judged good or bad.

Alex: And that matters because “LLM as judge” is getting more common, but a single score can be opaque.
Maya: Right. This paper says the feedback itself can help improve prompts too. So the judge is not just grading; it’s teaching.

Alex: That’s a big deal for teams using LLMs in summarization, support, or research workflows.
Maya: If you’ve ever wondered why a model keeps failing in one specific way, this kind of question-by-question evaluation gives you a debugging map.
